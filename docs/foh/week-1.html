Seven Tight End Claims, None Of Them Started, And He Lost By Half A Point.

Bob Dorsch, the defending champion, chased six different tight ends on the wire in Week 1. One claim landed. He started Sam LaPorta instead and lost 114.12 to 113.66. Two games in this league were decided by a combined eight tenths of a point.

On The Record

BOOMER: A call on every team, in the order I would rather own these rosters right now. Eleven on the board, none of them graded yet. Next week I read every one of them back, and when I am wrong I say so before I say anything else.

  1. Balls Deep. Spencer Lentz posts 150 or more again in Week 2.

    The only roster in this league with three starters over 30. If Hurts holds up, nobody here has the ceiling to trade punches with it.

  2. Colorado Narcoleptic Kestrels. Eric Olson starts Christian Watson in Week 2.

    Sixty-six career games here, fewer than anybody else in this league, and the highest career average in it at 116.63. He has never once stayed long enough for that to matter.

  3. Kim Jong Un Pleasure Squad. Aron Rogers leaves under five on his bench again.

    Jaxson Dart put up 32.6 for him. That was on nobody's list in August, and Rogers is the one man here who was never going to waste it.

  4. WPB Steel City. Bob Dorsch wins Week 2 with Pat Freiermuth in his lineup.

    He has won 61.3 percent of everything he has ever played here, and this league's own book calls him its best manager. He is 0 and 1 in the week he did more work than anybody and less with it than anybody. He has the answer now. Whether he plays it is all I am interested in.

  5. Napoleon's Army. Michael Turner loses another one by under five points before Halloween.

    He is 16 and 16 against Aaron Jezioro across 32 games, the longest rivalry in this league and still exactly level. Losing by 0.34 is not a coincidence for this man. It is a personality.

  6. Old Man Smashers. Ryan Kelly beats Aaron Jezioro by more than twenty.

    He has started the smallest share of the points he owned of anybody here, 85.4 percent of them, and he has four titles anyway. Nobody has ever been this successful while paying this little attention.

  7. Pack Up. Rahul Pahuja leaves twenty or more behind again, and this time it costs him.

    He won by 0.46 with 38.30 on his bench and released a 34.8-point receiver in the same week. Nobody gets away with that twice.

  8. Dallas Dawgs. Matt Davis gets a wide receiver into his top three scorers in Week 2.

    He swings less week to week than anybody in this league, and he has four runner-up finishes and three titles across two different websites. Nobody here has spent more time within one good Sunday of the trophy.

  9. I Loved You Tom Brady. Billy Norton scores 130 or more in Week 2.

    Nothing on that roster went wrong and nothing went right, which is the hardest kind of bad week to fix and the easiest to stop repeating. He gets Aron Rogers on Sunday, the man I just handed Owner of the Week. Four rings says he is not scared of him.

  10. Midget Grinders. Michael Smith beats Spencer Lentz on Sunday.

    Yes, the 94.4 against the 171.92, and yes, that means I need Smith to find fifty points he has found five times in fifteen years while I am also calling Lentz for 150. One of those two is going down and I have written both of them here in ink. Smith has been owed wins by this league since roughly the Obama administration, nearly seven of them by the arithmetic. Somewhere the tab comes due.

  11. | croup. Aaron Jezioro beats Ryan Kelly on Sunday.

    Yes, I know what I said five calls ago. Trevor Lawrence went for 34.1, the fifth-biggest start in the league, and everything else he put in the lineup added up to 54.1. A man with that quarterback and four titles across the table is exactly where I want to be wrong.

The Freiermuth Rule

BOOMER: New this year, and Bob Dorsch bought the naming rights in Week 1. Do not leave the answer on your own bench. I do not care whether you chased it across the wire seven times or it has been sitting on your roster since the auction. If you own it and it is not in your lineup, you broke the rule.

  1. Bob Dorsch. Seven claims to solve one slot. Solved it. Benched Pat Freiermuth and his 13.1. Lost by 0.46.
  2. Rahul Pahuja. Started Harold Fannin Jr. for 3.1 with Isaiah Likely and 23.8 in his pocket. Won by 0.46.

BOOMER: Same slot, same mistake, same afternoon, and the scoreboard handed one of them a W for it. That is this league in a sentence.
