Bob Dorsch Filed Seven Tight End Claims, Started None Of Them, And Lost By Half A Point.

The defending champion chased six different tight ends on the wire. One claim landed. He started Sam LaPorta instead and lost 114.12 to 113.66. Two games in this league were decided by a combined eight tenths of a point.

The whole week in 30 seconds

On The Record

BOOMER: Four calls, up top where nobody can say I hid them. Every one gets graded in this spot next week, in public, and when I am wrong I say so before I say anything else.

1

Bob Dorsch wins Week 2 with Pat Freiermuth in his lineup.

He has the answer now. He filed seven claims to get it. Whether he actually plays it is the only thing I am interested in.

2

Rahul Pahuja leaves twenty or more behind again, and this time it costs him.

He won by 0.46 with 38.30 on his bench and released a 34.8-point receiver in the same week. Nobody gets away with that twice.

3

Michael Turner loses another one by under five points before Halloween.

He owns the highest single week in the history of this league and he lost by a third of a point to a kicker. That is not a coincidence, that is a personality.

4

Billy Norton clears 130 in Week 2.

Four titles and a 108.84. DJ Moore, Dalton Kincaid and Ladd McConkey all cleared 16 and it still added up to nothing. That roster is better than that number.

GIL: Recorded, all four.

The Freiermuth Rule

BOOMER: New this year, and Bob Dorsch named it for us in week one. Do not leave the answer on your own bench. I do not care whether you chased it across the wire seven times or it has been sitting on your roster since the auction. If you own it and it is not in your lineup, you broke the rule.

  1. Bob Dorsch. Seven claims to solve one slot. Solved it. Benched Pat Freiermuth and his 13.1. Lost by 0.46.
  2. Rahul Pahuja. Started Harold Fannin Jr. for 3.1 with Isaiah Likely and 23.8 in his pocket. Won by 0.46.

BOOMER: Same slot, same mistake, same afternoon, and the scoreboard handed one of them a W for it. That is this league in a sentence.

Boomer's Power Rankings

BOOMER: Eleven teams. My order. One line each, and every line is something I have not already said.

  1. Balls Deep. The only roster in the league with three starters over 30. If Hurts holds up, nobody here has the ceiling to trade punches with it.
  2. Colorado Narcoleptic Kestrels. Eleven years away and he walks in with Josh Allen and Ashton Jeanty. Somebody explain to me how that happened.
  3. Kim Jong Un Pleasure Squad. Jaxson Dart put up 32.6 for him. That was not on anybody's list in August, and Rogers is the one man here who will not waste it.
  4. WPB Steel City. The best manager rating in this league at 61.3 percent lifetime, and he is 0 and 1 on the one week he stopped managing.
  5. Napoleon’s Army. Three starters over 22 and a kicker who cost him the game. Michael Turner is going to be furious all season and he has earned it.
  6. Old Man Smashers. Four rings, best drafter in the league, and the schedule gave him a week off to watch his quarterback throw for 41.
  7. Dallas Dawgs. Won a game in 2026 with two running backs and a defense. The receivers have to show up eventually or that is the season.
  8. Pack Up. 1 and 0 and the most wasted points in the league. He is playing with his eyes shut and it worked exactly once.
  9. I Loved You Tom Brady. Nothing on his roster went wrong and nothing went right. That is the hardest kind of bad week to fix.
  10. Midget Grinders. Jonathan Taylor at 23.6 and then a long quiet walk down the rest of the lineup.
  11. | croup. Started the fifth-biggest score in the league and finished last. Nobody manages to do both of those in the same afternoon.
